## Light and area decisions (correction block, 1.7.0-rc.1)

- Area brightness sensor: one optional Area property `area_illuminance_sensor`; priority Area sensor, global sensor, UNKNOWN; no fallback on failure; binary entities never used. Migration: none (additive key). Legacy `dark_entity` is ignored.
- Brightness limits are read also while the Light feature is disabled (parked configuration). To review: the limits are an Area brightness input, not light control.
- Disabled features keep their configuration in `retained_features` (inactive); re-enabling restores it. Additive key, no migration.
- A native light group that would switch an excluded lamp is not controlled as a whole and is refused at save.
- Automatic light calls are identified by their registered request context; a user context is never registered.

## Brightness classification (final)

- The Area brightness is one central source and one central classification. The Light feature consumes it; enabling or disabling the Light feature does not change it.
- Configured limits stay effective while the Light feature is disabled (parked configuration).
- No default limits exist in the code. Without configured limits the classification is UNKNOWN. UNKNOWN is never read as DARK or BRIGHT.
- The hysteresis band is kept. No new limits were introduced.

## Public illuminance threshold entity and central brightness (Phase 6, variant B)

- Two functions are kept apart. The central classification (`helpers/brightness.py`: DARK, BRIGHT, UNKNOWN from the Light Lux limits and the numeric Area illuminance sensor, then the global sensor) is the only source for Area brightness and light control. The public `light` threshold entity (`threshold.py`, native `ThresholdSensor`) is informational.
- Evidence: the threshold entity has no internal consumer. Light control, secondary states and the dashboard do not read it. Its configuration (Aggregation feature: `aggregates_illuminance_threshold`, hysteresis in percent) and its source (single illuminance sensor or the aggregate) differ from the Light Lux limits and the Area brightness source.
- Semantics differ, so there is no consolidation: the native entity switches ON strictly above `threshold + h` and OFF strictly below `threshold - h`, with an initial OFF inside the band and an unknown state when the reading is unavailable. The central classification switches DARK at `<= turn_on_below` and BRIGHT at `>= turn_off_above` (both inclusive), keeps the band only with a known previous state, and is UNKNOWN otherwise. `tests/test_brightness_threshold_comparison.py` pins the differences at the upper boundary (660) and the lower boundary (540).
- The public entity is kept unchanged: entity id, unique id, activation conditions, ON/OFF meaning, the native implementation. No migration, no removal, no change to its meaning.
- Documentation drift corrected: the threshold entity is not an input of the Area states, and `dark`/`bright` are no longer configured from a binary sensor.
- Public entities are treated as potentially used by external automations; this decision does not claim that such automations exist.

## Phase 7: UNKNOWN and Meta-Area decisions (decided, closed)

- UNKNOWN brightness, decided and closed: the four modes keep their distinct tasks. `ignore`: no brightness condition. `require_dark`: DARK is a necessary condition to switch on. `turn_off`: brightness is not a necessary condition to switch on; BRIGHT may trigger OFF. `dark_on_bright_off`: DARK is necessary to switch on; BRIGHT may trigger OFF.
- On a new occupation with UNKNOWN brightness: `ignore` and `turn_off` switch on when a switch is needed; `require_dark` and `dark_on_bright_off` do not switch on automatically. Activation, blockers, override, ownership and the actual need to switch still apply. A sensor outage alone never sends a command. UNKNOWN is neither DARK nor BRIGHT. The event-dependent BRIGHT semantics of `turn_off` and the rules for sensor recovery and CLEAR are unchanged.
- The difference to 1.6.18 is intended and accepted. The code is not changed to match 1.6.18.
- Meta-Area brightness, decided and closed: at least one relevant DARK child gives DARK; all relevant children BRIGHT give BRIGHT; no DARK and at least one UNKNOWN give UNKNOWN; no relevant children give UNKNOWN. A relevant child without a brightness source is UNKNOWN and is never ignored or treated as BRIGHT. A BRIGHT child together with other relevant UNKNOWN children gives UNKNOWN. Exactly one relevant BRIGHT child gives BRIGHT (test `test_exactly_one_relevant_bright_child_is_bright`).
- Public illuminance threshold entity: unchanged, Variant B (see above).

## GlobalEntry identity and lifecycle (after RC.3 field test, 2026-11-26)

- Ownership of generated entities is the Area's Adaptive Areas device (stable Area ID). Unique-ID name parts are not used. No unique-ID or entity-ID migration.
- Consumers of generated entities resolve the registered entity ID by unique ID; the light control switch is read as the Area's runtime object. No parallel registry.
- Deleting an Area removes its generated entities and device during the following setup; devices of Areas no longer configured are removed the same way (one rule, idempotent). Device-less generated entities in a GlobalEntry and devices that also hold foreign entities are left unchanged.
- A missing Home Assistant Area or floor skips that Area and raises `missing_area`; configuration and entities are kept.
- Phase F: the slug of an Area is read back from its registered presence state entity, so renames keep all IDs; a new Area whose slug is taken uses its Area ID (then a type suffix, then a counter). No migration: existing registered IDs are reused as they are.
- Phase F: Area Climate reads the registered Health sensor by unique ID, without fallback to a default ID.
- Phase F: the per-Area options flow is not offered for the GlobalEntry (`async_supports_options_flow`).
- Open, needs a decision: an Area and a floor with the same ID cannot both live in `options[areas]` (one key space). Lifting this needs a schema migration of the stored keys; not done.
